Best 60% Keyboards 2026

60% keyboards drop the function row, navigation cluster, and numpad — leaving just the essentials. We tested 22 of them across gaming, typing, and programming. These are the ones worth your money.

Affiliate disclosure: This article contains affiliate links. Read our disclosure.

What you're giving up (and gaining) with 60%

You lose: arrow keys (usually), function row, Home/End/PgUp/PgDn, numpad. Most 60% boards require a Fn layer for these — a secondary function triggered by holding Fn + another key.

You gain: 40% less desk space, more room for mouse movement (huge for FPS gamers), cleaner aesthetic, more portable.

If you're a heavy user of arrows or Home/End, consider a 65% or 75% keyboard instead. If you can adapt to a Fn layer, 60% is glorious.

What switch type should you pick?

  • Linear (Red): Smooth, no bump. Best for gaming. Examples: Gateron Yellow, Cherry MX Red, Kailh Box Red.
  • Tactile (Brown): Small bump on actuation, no click. Best for typing and general use. Examples: Cherry MX Brown, Holy Panda.
  • Clicky (Blue): Tactile bump + audible click. Satisfying but loud — avoid if you have roommates or work in shared spaces.
  • Hall-effect (Magnetic): Adjustable actuation. Best for competitive FPS. Wooting and SteelSeries use these.

Layout considerations

True 60% boards (no arrows) require the biggest adjustment. If you can spend $10-20 more, look for "60% with arrows" or "65% layout" — you get the same desk space savings but keep arrow keys.

Frequently asked questions

Are 60% keyboards good for gaming?

Excellent for gaming, and they are the competitive standard because the compact layout leaves more desk space for mouse movement, which is a real advantage in a shooter. The trade-off is no function row, no arrow keys, and no dedicated navigation cluster. Most 60% boards solve this with layers or arrow key combinations, which is worth learning before you commit.

What is the best 60% mechanical keyboard?

The Wooting 60HE is our best 60% for competitive gaming because of its 8K polling, rapid trigger, and hall effect magnetic switches that let you set your own actuation point. For general use, the GMMK Pro is our best overall because of its sound, build, and full customisation. The Royal Kludge RK61 is the best budget option by a wide margin.

Is a 60% keyboard comfortable for typing?

Yes, provided the layout is right. A 60% board with a proper bottom row spacing and a spacebar that is not excessively long types very well. The real issue is that without a function row you lose the dedicated F1-F12 keys, which matters more for productivity than for gaming. See our typing guide for full-size and 75% alternatives.

Do 60% keyboards need arrow keys?

Most quality 60% boards provide arrow keys through a Fn layer, typically mapped to IJKL or HJKL. The Keychron and GMMK boards in our mechanical keyboard guide all support this. If you use arrow keys constantly for editing, a 65% or 75% layout is a better fit.

Are 60% keyboards good for travel?

They are the best option for travel because they are genuinely small and most fit in a laptop bag sleeve. Pair one with a lightweight mouse and you have a complete portable setup. Note that many 60% boards have non-standard bottom rows, so check your existing keyboard shortcuts before switching.
