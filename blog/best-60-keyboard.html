Best 60% Keyboards 2026

60% keyboards drop the function row, navigation cluster, and numpad — leaving just the essentials. We tested 22 of them across gaming, typing, and programming. These are the ones worth your money.

Affiliate disclosure: This article contains affiliate links. Read our disclosure.

What you're giving up (and gaining) with 60%

You lose: arrow keys (usually), function row, Home/End/PgUp/PgDn, numpad. Most 60% boards require a Fn layer for these — a secondary function triggered by holding Fn + another key.

You gain: 40% less desk space, more room for mouse movement (huge for FPS gamers), cleaner aesthetic, more portable.

If you're a heavy user of arrows or Home/End, consider a 65% or 75% keyboard instead. If you can adapt to a Fn layer, 60% is glorious.

1. Wooting 60HE — Best for Competitive Gaming

The Wooting 60HE uses Hall-effect (magnetic) switches that let you adjust the actuation point anywhere from 0.1mm to 4.0mm in software. Combined with Rapid Trigger (reset based on travel, not fixed distance), it gives a tangible competitive edge in Valorant, CS2, and any game with movement mechanics.

The keyboard itself is solid — aluminum case, screw-in stabilizers, lubed Lekker switches. PBT keycaps are dye-sublimated, will last for years. Software is straightforward, no account required.

What switch type should you pick?

  • Linear (Red): Smooth, no bump. Best for gaming. Examples: Gateron Yellow, Cherry MX Red, Kailh Box Red.
  • Tactile (Brown): Small bump on actuation, no click. Best for typing and general use. Examples: Cherry MX Brown, Holy Panda.
  • Clicky (Blue): Tactile bump + audible click. Satisfying but loud — avoid if you have roommates or work in shared spaces.
  • Hall-effect (Magnetic): Adjustable actuation. Best for competitive FPS. Wooting and SteelSeries use these.

Layout considerations

True 60% boards (no arrows) require the biggest adjustment. If you can spend $10-20 more, look for "60% with arrows" or "65% layout" — you get the same desk space savings but keep arrow keys.

Final recommendation

For most people, the Keychron Q1 Pro is the right answer. For competitive FPS, the Wooting 60HE is in a different league. Both are excellent.
