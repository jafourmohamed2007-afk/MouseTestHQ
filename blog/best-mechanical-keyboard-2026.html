Understanding switch types

Before buying, understand the three main switch families:

  • Linear (Red): Smooth, no bump. Best for gaming. Examples: Gateron Yellow, Cherry MX Red.
  • Tactile (Brown): Small bump on actuation, no click. Best for typing. Examples: Cherry MX Brown, Holy Panda.
  • Clicky (Blue): Tactile bump + audible click. Satisfying but loud. Examples: Cherry MX Blue, Kailh Box White.

Combined with Rapid Trigger (reset and re-actuate based on travel, not fixed distance), the 80HE gives a tangible edge in competitive FPS. Build quality is solid, the sound profile is creamy thanks to lubed Lekker switches.

How to choose the right layout

  • Full-size (104 keys): Numpad included. Best if you work with numbers.
  • TKL (87 keys): No numpad, saves desk space. Most popular among gamers.
  • 75% (84 keys): TKL but compact — function row + arrows in less space.
  • 65% (68 keys): No function row. Use a Fn layer for F-keys.
  • 60% (61 keys): Minimalist, requires Fn layer for arrows. Takes getting used to.
  • Split / ergonomic: Reduces strain during long typing sessions.

Testing for ghosting & N-key rollover

Before gaming on any new keyboard, run our Keyboard Tester and try the anti-ghosting combo test. Most modern keyboards offer NKRO over USB, but cheap boards often fail at 3+ keys. The Wooting 80HE passed all 10 of our stress combinations; the Logitech MX Mechanical passed 9/10.

Modding: is it worth it?

If you buy a hot-swap keyboard (Q1 Pro, K2, Drop CTRL, etc.), you can swap switches without soldering. Common mods:

  • Switch lube: Krytox 205g0 makes linear switches smoother and quieter. ~$15 and an hour of work.
  • PBT keycaps: Drop or KBDfans PBT sets feel better and resist shine. $40–80.
  • Foam mod: Adding foam inside the case changes the sound profile. Pre-installed on many newer boards.
  • Tape mod: Electrical tape on the PCB for a slightly different bottom-out feel.

If you know your preferred layout, our best 60% keyboards and best TKL keyboards guides go deeper. Typing-focused buyers should read best keyboard for typing for switch and sound advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best mechanical keyboard in 2026?

The Keychron Q1 Pro is our best overall pick. It has a gasket-mounted aluminium case, hot-swappable sockets, excellent factory lubing and sound, and it is available in both 75% and 80% layouts. For pure gaming performance, the Wooting 80HE wins because of its class-leading 8K polling and rapid trigger. For typing, see our dedicated typing guide.

Are linear, tactile or switches best for gaming?

Linear switches are the standard choice for competitive gaming because they actuate immediately with no tactile bump, and they rarely produce accidental presses. Tactile switches are a good middle ground if you like feedback. For typing, a heavier tactile or a well-lubed linear is usually the most comfortable. Our maintenance guide covers keeping switches clean.

Is a hot-swappable keyboard worth it?

Yes, if you plan to customise. Hot-swap sockets let you change switches with a puller and no soldering, which makes it easy to try different types and to fix a single failed switch. If you will keep one switch type forever and prefer a specific feel, a soldered board is cheaper and can sound marginally better. Our 60% keyboard guide covers which of our picks are hot-swap.

Do I need a gasket mount?

It is not essential, but it is the single biggest contributor to the flexible, muted typing feel that premium boards deliver. A gasket mount uses a soft gasket between the plate and case to absorb vibration, which reduces the hollow ping sound. Most of the boards we recommend in our typing guide use it.

How do I check for ghosting and N-key rollover?

Use our free online keyboard tester. It highlights stuck keys and runs an anti-ghosting rollover test so you can confirm your board registers every key combination you press simultaneously. Boards with NKRO support the most simultaneous keys, which matters for gaming modifiers and shortcuts.
