Best TKL Keyboards 2026

Tenkeyless (TKL) keyboards drop only the numpad, keeping the function row and arrow keys. It's the most popular layout for gamers and office workers. We tested 16 TKL boards — these are the picks.

Affiliate disclosure: This article contains affiliate links. Read our disclosure.

Why TKL is the sweet spot

TKL gives you:

  • Numpad removed — saves ~10cm of desk space, mouse has more room
  • Function row kept — F-keys work without Fn layer gymnastics
  • Arrow keys kept — essential for most users, including gamers
  • Navigation cluster kept — Home, End, PgUp, PgDn are useful for productivity

You're giving up only the numpad. If you don't enter numbers all day, this is the right size.

1. Wooting 80HE — Best for Competitive Gaming

The Wooting 80HE is the most advanced TKL keyboard ever made. Hall-effect switches let you adjust actuation anywhere from 0.1mm to 4.0mm. Rapid Trigger resets based on travel distance, not fixed position.

The result: meaningful competitive advantage in any movement-heavy game. The build quality is excellent — aluminum case, screw-in stabilizers, PBT keycaps. Software is offline-capable, no account required.

Switch types explained

Linear: Smooth, no bump. Fastest. Best for gaming. Examples: Gateron Yellow, Cherry MX Red, GX Red.

Tactile: Small bump on actuation. Best for typing. Examples: Cherry MX Brown, GX Brown.

Clicky: Tactile + audible click. Loud, satisfying. Examples: Cherry MX Blue, GX Blue.

Optical: Light-based actuation. Faster than mechanical. Examples: Razer Optical, Logitech GX Optical.

Hall-effect (magnetic): Adjustable actuation. Newest tech. Examples: Wooting Lekker, SteelSeries Omnipoint.

Frequently asked questions

Is a TKL keyboard better than a full-size?

For most people, yes. TKL gives you a function row and dedicated arrow keys, which is everything most people actually use from a full-size board, while removing the numpad that takes up desk space and gets in the way of mouse movement. If you do heavy data entry or work in spreadsheets, keep the numpad. Our full comparison covers this in detail.

What is the best TKL mechanical keyboard in 2026?

The Keychron Q1 Pro in TKL form is our best overall. It has a gasket-mounted aluminium case, hot-swap sockets, and a muted, flexible sound. For competitive gaming, the Wooting 80HE is faster because of its 8K polling and rapid trigger. The Keychron K8 Pro is the best value TKL we tested.

Are TKL keyboards good for gaming?

Yes, and they are arguably the best balance for gaming that is not a full competitive 60%. You get arrow keys for games that need them, a function row, and still save meaningful desk space over a full-size. The only real downside versus a 60% is slightly less mouse space, which matters more in FPS games than in MMOs. See our FPS mouse guide for pairing advice.

Should I get a TKL or a 75% keyboard?

TKL if you want the largest possible mouse area and are used to 87 keys. 75% if you want a number row for shortcuts and work, but want the board to stay compact. Most people who try a 75% end up happy with it. Both are covered in our main keyboard guide.

How do I keep a TKL keyboard clean?

Flip it upside down, shake out debris, and use a brush or a soft brush attachment with a vacuum for the gaps between keys and around the switches. For sticky keys or grime on the keycaps, our full cleaning guide has the step-by-step process, including how to safely remove keycaps.
