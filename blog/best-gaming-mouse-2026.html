How to choose the right mouse for you

Three things matter most, in order:

  1. Shape and size. The mouse has to fit your hand and grip. Use our testing tools to identify your grip (palm, claw, fingertip) and measure your hand (tip of middle finger to base of palm). Match against mouse dimensions.
  2. Weight. For FPS games, lighter is better. Under 65g is the modern standard. For productivity or MMO, weight matters less than button count.
  3. Sensor accuracy. All mice on this list have excellent sensors. Don't worry about this unless you're buying a $20 no-name mouse.

What about polling rate?

1000Hz is the practical sweet spot. Anything higher (4000Hz, 8000Hz) requires a high-end CPU to deliver consistently, and the perceptible benefit is minimal. Test your actual rate with our polling rate checker — many "high-polling" mice drop to 500Hz on busier USB hubs or front-panel headers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is wireless really as good as wired now?

For 99% of users, yes. Modern wireless mice add 1–4ms of latency over wired — a difference that's not perceptible in any game. The convenience and clean desk are worth it.

Do I need 8000Hz polling?

Probably not. Unless you're playing at a very high level (ranked pro or top 1% of any competitive game), the difference between 1000Hz and 8000Hz is below the threshold of perception.

What's the best grip?

There's no universal best — use whichever feels most natural. Palm grip is most relaxed; claw grip is most precise; fingertip grip is most agile. Test with our CPS test to see which works best for your hand.

How long do gaming mice last?

Premium mice last 5+ years for typical use. Mechanical switches are rated for 50–80 million clicks; optical switches are rated 100 million+ and don't degrade over time. Most "mouse failures" are double-click issues from worn mechanical switches.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best gaming mouse for 2026?

The Logitech G Pro X Superlight 2 is our best overall pick for 2026. It is the only mouse in our test group that scored well on every axis: a flawless 26,000 DPI HERO 2 sensor, 60g weight, 95-hour battery, and reliable 8000Hz wireless. If you need 8000Hz polling specifically, the Endgame Gear OP1 8k is the better choice, and if you want zero software and maximum polling rate at the lowest latency, the Zowie EC2-CW wins on raw responsiveness.

Is a lighter mouse always better for gaming?

No. Lighter mice (under 60g) are usually better for competitive shooters where you make fast, frequent lifts and flicks, because they reduce fatigue over long sessions. For slower-paced games, strategy titles, or hybrid work, a 70-80g mouse often feels more stable because the extra weight damps hand tremor and stops accidental micro-movements. The honest answer is that weight preference is personal, which is why we recommend testing two mice side by side before committing.

Does polling rate actually make a difference in games?

Marginal in practice, and much smaller than the marketing suggests. Going from 125Hz to 1000Hz produces a real but small improvement in input-to-photon latency. Going from 1000Hz to 8000Hz is measurable in a lab instrument but rarely noticeable in a game, because your monitor refresh rate and the game engine become the bottleneck. Our polling rate explainer breaks down exactly where the gains stop mattering.

What DPI should I use for competitive FPS games?

Most pros in Valorant and CS2 use 400-800 DPI at low in-game sensitivity, which gives them a low, consistent sens that minimises arm movement and micro-correction noise. What matters is the total sensitivity you end up with, not the DPI number itself. Our CPI vs DPI guide explains why these two terms are confusing and how to pick a sens that works for you.

Should I buy a wired or wireless gaming mouse?

Wireless is now the default recommendation in 2026 for most players. Modern wireless protocols have essentially eliminated the input lag gap, and the freedom to move without a cable has become a genuine quality-of-life improvement at work as well as in game. Wired is still worth it if you play at the absolute highest polling rates, sit at a multi-monitor desk setup where the cable is a nuisance, or want to guarantee zero dropouts.
