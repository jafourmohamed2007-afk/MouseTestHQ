How to choose the right mouse for you

Three things matter most, in order:

  1. Shape and size. The mouse has to fit your hand and grip. Use our testing tools to identify your grip (palm, claw, fingertip) and measure your hand (tip of middle finger to base of palm). Match against mouse dimensions.
  2. Weight. For FPS games, lighter is better. Under 65g is the modern standard. For productivity or MMO, weight matters less than button count.
  3. Sensor accuracy. All mice on this list have excellent sensors. Don't worry about this unless you're buying a $20 no-name mouse.

What about polling rate?

1000Hz is the practical sweet spot. Anything higher (4000Hz, 8000Hz) requires a high-end CPU to deliver consistently, and the perceptible benefit is minimal. Test your actual rate with our polling rate checker — many "high-polling" mice drop to 500Hz on busier USB hubs or front-panel headers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is wireless really as good as wired now?

For 99% of users, yes. Modern wireless mice add 1–4ms of latency over wired — a difference that's not perceptible in any game. The convenience and clean desk are worth it.

Do I need 8000Hz polling?

Probably not. Unless you're playing at a very high level (ranked pro or top 1% of any competitive game), the difference between 1000Hz and 8000Hz is below the threshold of perception.

What's the best grip?

There's no universal best — use whichever feels most natural. Palm grip is most relaxed; claw grip is most precise; fingertip grip is most agile. Test with our CPS test to see which works best for your hand.

How long do gaming mice last?

Premium mice last 5+ years for typical use. Mechanical switches are rated for 50–80 million clicks; optical switches are rated 100 million+ and don't degrade over time. Most "mouse failures" are double-click issues from worn mechanical switches.
