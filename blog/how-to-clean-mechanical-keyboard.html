How to Clean a Mechanical Keyboard

A properly maintained mechanical keyboard lasts 10+ years. A neglected one develops sticky keys, scratchy switches, and pet hair in places you'd rather not think about. Here's the full cleaning routine.

What you'll need

  • Keycap puller (usually included with mechanical keyboards, $2-5 if not)
  • Switch puller (for hot-swap boards only, $3-8)
  • Compressed air ($8-15, lasts years)
  • Soft brush (paint brush, makeup brush, or electronics cleaning brush)
  • Microfiber cloth (lint-free)
  • Mild soap (dish soap works) or isopropyl alcohol (90%+)
  • Bowl of warm water (for keycaps only)
  • Small Phillips screwdriver (for disassembly if needed)
  • USB cable / port access for testing keys after cleaning

The 5-minute weekly routine

Do this every week to prevent buildup:

  1. Unplug the keyboard (or turn off if wireless)
  2. Turn it upside down over a trash can and shake gently — large debris falls out
  3. Use compressed air between every key, holding the can upright (tilted cans spit liquid)
  4. Wipe down the keycaps with a slightly damp microfiber cloth
  5. Done

This takes 5 minutes and prevents 90% of deeper cleaning needs.

The 30-minute monthly deep clean

  1. Remove all keycaps using the keycap puller. Pull straight up — don't twist or angle.
  2. Soak keycaps in warm soapy water for 15-20 minutes. Agitate gently.
  3. Rinse thoroughly with clean water. Any soap residue will feel sticky when dry.
  4. Air dry completely on a towel. Minimum 2 hours, ideally overnight.
  5. With caps removed, blow out the keyboard with compressed air. Hold at 45° angle to avoid blowing debris deeper into switches.
  6. Use the brush to dislodge anything stuck in the gaps.
  7. Wipe the exposed plate with a slightly damp cloth.
  8. Test the keyboard with our Keyboard Tester to verify all keys still register.
  9. Replace keycaps once fully dry.

Cleaning switches (hot-swap boards only)

If you have a hot-swap board and a switch feels scratchy or inconsistent:

  1. Remove the switch with a switch puller. Pull straight up, gently rocking if stuck.
  2. Inspect for debris, bent pins, or damage.
  3. For light cleaning: blow compressed air into the housing.
  4. For deep cleaning: carefully disassemble the switch (top housing → spring → stem → bottom housing) and clean each part with 99% isopropyl alcohol. Let dry fully (10+ minutes) before reassembling.
  5. Reinstall the switch. Pins go into the PCB holes — match the orientation markings.
  6. Test the key with our Keyboard Tester before continuing to the next switch.

If a switch is mechanically damaged, just replace it — new switches are $0.30-0.80 each.

What NOT to do

  • Don't spray cleaner directly on the keyboard — liquid can get inside switches and short the PCB
  • Don't use bleach or harsh chemicals — destroys keycap legends and plastic
  • Don't put keycaps in the dishwasher — heat warps them
  • Don't pull keycaps at angles — breaks the stem
  • Don't try to fix sticky switches with WD-40 — attracts dust, makes it worse
  • Don't soak mechanical keycaps in hot water — warps ABS plastic. Use warm (not hot) water.

Cleaning by switch type

ABS keycaps (most stock caps): develop shiny spots over time. Soap and water work; legends may fade with aggressive scrubbing.

PBT keycaps (most aftermarket): more durable, legends don't fade, can take stronger cleaning.

Pudding keycaps (transparent sides): clean similarly to opaque. Avoid alcohol on the legends.

How often should you clean?

  • Weekly quick clean: 5 minutes, prevents buildup
  • Monthly deep clean: 30 minutes, removes grime and debris
  • Quarterly full maintenance: switch inspection, lubrication if needed, full keycap wash
  • After spills: immediately — power off, disconnect, blot with microfiber, let dry 48 hours before testing

Cleaning wireless keyboards

Same process, but extra caution:

  • Power off completely (don't just disconnect)
  • Protect the charging port — don't get liquid near it
  • Don't submerge the keyboard even briefly
  • Some sealed boards (like the Logitech MX Mechanical) have IPX ratings — check yours before cleaning

Final tips

Mechanical keyboards are built to be cleaned and maintained. A well-cared-for keyboard from 2016 still types better than most new ones. Set a calendar reminder for monthly deep cleans and your keyboard will outlive your next three computers.

Before cleaning, run our Keyboard Tester and screenshot which keys feel "off" — this gives you a baseline to verify your cleaning fixed any issues.
