How We Test Mice & Keyboards

Every recommendation on MouseTestHQ is backed by real measurements, not marketing specs. This page explains exactly how we test mice, keyboards, and peripherals — the equipment, the processes, and the criteria we score against.

We publish this so you can judge our conclusions yourself. If you disagree with a pick, you have the tools to verify.

Our testing philosophy

We follow three principles:

  1. Measure what matters. Sensor accuracy, polling rate stability, click latency, and switch feel — not headline specs.
  2. Test under real conditions. Hours of use, not benchmarks. A mouse that's "perfect on paper" but uncomfortable after 2 hours is a bad mouse.
  3. Be transparent. Every review includes what we tested, how long, and any limitations of our method.

How we measure mouse performance

1. Polling rate stability

We measure polling rate over 10 minutes of continuous mouse movement, sampling every 100ms. Stable mice hold their rated polling rate (usually 1000Hz) with less than 5% deviation. Unstable mice dip to 500Hz or 250Hz frequently.

Our tool: Our free Polling Rate Checker in your browser gives you the same data we collect.

2. Click latency

We measure the time from physical switch actuation to the click event reaching the operating system. Lower is better. Healthy modern mice: 5-8ms. Worn or cheap switches: 12ms+.

Method: High-speed camera (1000 fps) records switch press; software correlates the timestamp with the USB HID event. We measure 100 clicks per mouse and take the median.

3. Sensor accuracy

Modern sensors (HERO 2, Focus Pro 30K, PMW3950) are all excellent, but we verify on a controlled motion rig. The mouse moves at consistent speeds across a 30cm track; we measure reported movement vs actual.

Pass criteria: less than 1% deviation across all tested speeds (10-200 IPS).

4. Battery life

For wireless mice, we measure continuous-use battery life at rated polling rate. Real-world battery life is usually 80-90% of manufacturer claims due to LED lighting, higher polling, and other factors.

5. Wireless consistency

We test wireless mice in environments with 2.4GHz WiFi, Bluetooth devices, and microwaves (common home/office interference). The mouse must hold its rated polling rate without dropouts.

Keyboard testing equipment

1. Switch analysis

We disassemble sample switches and inspect:

  • Spring weight (measured on calibrated force gauge)
  • Actuation point (measured with custom rig)
  • Stem wobble
  • Factory lubing quality

2. Typing feel

Subjective but important. Our testing panel of 12 writers/coders/types 5,000+ words per keyboard across 2 weeks. We score on:

  • Tactility (is the bump clear or mushy?)
  • Sound (clean thock vs rattly vs pingy)
  • Bottom-out feel (smooth vs harsh)
  • Stabilizer smoothness (spacebar, enter, shift — common rattle points)

3. Anti-ghosting & rollover

We use our Keyboard Tester (same tool you have) to verify NKRO (N-key rollover). Modern keyboards should handle 10+ simultaneous keys; cheap boards often fail at 3-4 keys pressed together.

4. Sound measurement

We record keyboard sound at 30cm distance using a calibrated decibel meter, both at typing speed (60 WPM) and during fast gaming inputs. This gives a consistent, comparable sound profile.

Subjective scoring criteria

Some things can't be measured with equipment. We score subjectively on:

  • Comfort — long-session fatigue (1-10 hours)
  • Build quality — flex, creak, weight distribution
  • Aesthetics — design language, RGB (if any), branding restraint
  • Software — usability, required vs optional, cloud dependence

What we don't test

Honesty about limitations:

  • We don't have an anechoic chamber for precise audio measurements — sound scores are relative, not absolute
  • We can't test every hand size perfectly — we use 12 testers but the range is limited to 15.5-22cm hand length
  • We don't test long-term durability (years) — reviews reflect 1-3 month testing windows. Some products need years to reveal issues (like double-clicking mechanical switches).

Editorial independence

We are not paid by manufacturers for coverage. Affiliate links generate revenue when you buy through them, but the commission rate never affects our rankings. A 1% commission product that's the best for the use case will rank above a 6% commission product that's mediocre.
